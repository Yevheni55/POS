=== SUMMARIES ===
--- components-theme ---
The components/theme subsystem is in good shape overall: Dialogs.kt is a genuine shared dialog library (confirm, PIN gate, storno, payment, split, shift close) with consistent busy-state gating, Theme.kt centralizes the Warm Hearth palette including the M3 surfaceContainer ladder override, and Motion.kt provides a documented motion-token system with reduced-motion gating on loops and tickers. The main debts are: the M3 colorScheme leaves secondaryContainer/tertiary at stock Material baseline so FilterChips in NoteDialog render off-brand lavender; the PIN pad is implemented three separate times (Dialogs, LoginScreen, DochadzkaTerminal) with already-divergent behavior; the active-toggle pill pattern is copy-pasted in at least 7 places; and the Warm Hearth palette has no documented token source — DESIGN-CODE.md still describes the old dark/purple POS system, so "token fidelity" is unverifiable by the project's own rules. There is also asset hygiene risk: the new Plus Jakarta/Instrument Serif fonts are untracked in git while Theme.kt referencing them is modified, and ~550 KB of orphaned Sora/Manrope TTFs ship in the APK with no resource shrinking enabled. Remaining issues are polish-level (device-TZ header clock, string-sniffed snackbar severity, locale-dependent String.format money formatting, partial reduced-motion coverage).
--- admin-ui-1 ---
The native Admin (shell + 14 native pages + 3 WebView fallbacks) is a faithful, well-commented port of the web admin with a consistent loading/error/empty pattern, confirm dialogs on destructive actions, and good Drizzle string-vs-number DTO discipline. The shell is simple and solid (enum-driven rail, phone dropdown nav), but navigation state and data are fully discarded on every page switch — everything refetches, and the three WebView fallback pages share one composable slot so switching between them silently does not navigate (a real bug). Per-page quality is good for tablets-landscape but several layouts (Pohyby filter bar, two-panel Menu/Recipes, 3-up KPI rows) will clip or cramp on phones and portrait tablets despite v2.2 phone support. First-real-data risks: unpaginated write-offs rendered in a non-lazy column, movement names falling back to "ID: N" for inactive ingredients and menu-item sales, silent zero-fill in the weekly revenue chart, and number keyboards that may block negative/decimal stock adjustments. Duplication is significant but bounded: roughly 800-1200 lines of dropdowns, segmented controls, modal scaffolds, empty states and EUR formatters are reimplemented per page instead of living in AdminUi.kt.
--- admin-ui-2 ---
The 12 remaining admin pages are overall solid: each page consistently implements loading/error/empty states (LoadingBox/ErrorBox/EmptyHint), money goes through the sk-SK fmtCost/money helpers, timestamps are pinned to Europe/Bratislava in nearly all formatters, and every fiscal-grade destructive action (STORNO, change payment method, re-fiscalization, storno-basket delete) sits behind an explicit AdminConfirm with clear Slovak copy. DTO comments documenting Drizzle string-vs-number quirks are a real strength. The two worst defects are structural UI bugs: StaffScreen renders its card list inside a non-scrollable container (employees below the fold are unreachable) and PaymentsScreen gives every table row its own independent horizontal scroll state under a fixed-width non-scrolling header (columns misalign per row). Beyond that there is a cluster of medium issues: optimistic undo-delete flows that can silently drop the server DELETE, Cashflow date presets computed in UTC instead of Bratislava (wrong day after midnight, exactly when a beach bar closes out), per-keystroke reloads without request sequencing, and unconfirmed adjacent stock-mutation buttons in the storno basket. There is also notable copy-paste duplication (three fake FlowRow implementations, two undo toasts, three inline Bratislava formatters) and several layouts that will overflow on the officially supported sub-600dp phones.
--- arch-core ---
The Android core is a deliberately minimal single-Activity Compose app: no ViewModels or DI, state lives in composable-local `remember` vars backed by two singletons (Mem in-memory cache, Store = JSON-in-SharedPreferences for offline cache/drafts/queues) and AppPrefs. Config-change survival is achieved by suppressing recreation via manifest configChanges, and process death is handled surprisingly well (Navigation restore guard, per-table draft persistence, idempotency keys on order sync and payments). The payment/fiscal pipeline is the strongest part — timeout-vs-connect-failure distinction, idempotent retry, conservative paragon fallback. The main weaknesses are: a real cart-loss race between syncToServer's bulk cart clear and unguarded product taps; money/print orchestration living in UI-scoped coroutines inside a ~1700-line OrderScreen composable (untestable, cancellable); lifecycle-unaware 10–15 s polling loops; and a weak security posture (plaintext JWT in backed-up SharedPreferences, global cleartext HTTP, unverified APK self-update over HTTP).
--- networking ---
The Android networking layer is a clean, modern stack: Retrofit 2.11 + OkHttp 4.12 + kotlinx-serialization (lenient, coerceInputValues, defaults everywhere), a dynamic base-URL interceptor over AppPrefs, Bearer-token auth interceptor, suspend calls always wrapped in Dispatchers.IO (no main-thread network), and an unusually thoughtful payments flow that distinguishes connect-failure vs timeout vs HTTP errors and mirrors the web fiscal-outcome state machine. Idempotency keys protect order creation, item adds and payments; an offline queue with replay semantics covers item deletions and storno reasons; a two-tier cache (Mem in-memory + SharedPreferences) gives instant screens with offline fallback. The serious gaps are concentrated in rare-but-costly failure windows: syncToServer can return null/stale after a *successful* createOrder, silently no-opping payment and inviting cart re-entry; the immediate same-key payment retry on timeout can race the still-running original request because the server persists the idempotency key only at response time; and the paragon (legal fallback document) endpoint has no idempotency key at all. Secondary debt: Mem cache is never cleared across logout/server switch, everything is cleartext HTTP with the JWT in backup-eligible SharedPreferences, and table/order freshness relies on 10–15 s polling with several sequential GETs per tick.
--- pos-ui ---
The cashier-facing UI is functionally rich and unusually careful about money-path correctness: idempotency keys on cart-sync and payment, timeout retry with the same key, a paragon fallback only on provable non-delivery, offline queues with proper locking, and per-table draft persistence. The is-primary phase state machine (exactly one glowing CTA) and Mem-cache instant transitions match the owner's speed priorities well. The main weaknesses are: (1) two real input bugs — the hold-to-repeat stepper fires one extra step on release, and the Dochadzka confirmation splash does not block touches, allowing accidental clock-out; (2) OrderScreen is a 1456-line god composable with ~40 state vars and all business logic inline, which combined with Kotlin 1.9.24 (no strong skipping) makes nearly the whole screen recompose on every cart tap and every 10 s poll tick; (3) several sub-44dp tap targets in the most-used row of the app violate the project's own design code. Phone layouts are mostly solid (rememberSaveable tab, adaptive grids), with one overflow in the attendance shifts overlay.
--- server-contract ---
The Android↔Express contract is in unusually good shape at the wire level: all 37 kasa endpoints in Api.kt plus the ~70 admin-screen endpoints exist on the server with matching paths, payload fields, and response shapes; numeric-as-string quirks, top-level-null pitfalls, and print-dest string matching are all handled deliberately, and the idempotency-key design for cart sync and payments is genuinely robust against retry-after-timeout duplication. Auth is PIN→12h HS256 JWT via Bearer header with a DB-backed lockout, and error bodies follow a consistent {error} format the app parses everywhere. The one systemic defect is the privilege model: the client's manager-PIN gate (verify-manager) returns no credential, while storno, discount, storno-basket, and table-layout endpoints are JWT-role-gated server-side — so for a cashier-role session every 'manager-approved' action still 403s after a correct PIN, and the offline storno queue then silently discards those records (data loss). Around that sit resilience gaps (shift-close skipped on any summary fetch error, 401 handled only on the floor screen, cashier sessions hammering a manager-only z-report endpoint) and the absence of any API versioning, with concrete client/server drift already present in the dead 422/overrideLimit path. Transport is cleartext HTTP permitted to any host with the JWT in plain SharedPreferences — acceptable over Tailscale, but not scoped to it.
